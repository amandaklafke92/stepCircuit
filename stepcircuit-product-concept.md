# StepCircuit — workshop product concept

Captured 28 September 2026 for the UTS Startups / Build Club AI Build Sprint. This is a working product concept to test, not a finished product specification.

## Product name and description

**StepCircuit** is the name Amanda chose for both this project and its proposed kit. Her personal wearable experiment will inform the kit.

> **Build your own step tracker.** A beginner-friendly kit for making a wearable you can use every day, with clear visual instructions and optional AI help along the way.

“Use every day” is the intended benefit, not a verified claim about the current prototype. The kit's possible customer is someone new to electronics who wants to make something useful, understand how it works, and own their step data. This customer and their willingness to pay need testing. The final wearable form factor, components, price, and sales plan are open.

## What the kit might include

- Hardware and device software for a wearable step counter, with access to step data without a required subscription.
- IKEA-style, 3D-modelled visual instructions showing how to assemble the actual hardware. Amanda wants to make these after building and verifying her own device.
- Programming guidance so a beginner can set up and check the device. The format is still open.

The product may eventually use optional AI guidance to help buyers learn or troubleshoot, while Amanda will also use AI during development. **AI support is outside the first MVP.** The step counter itself does not need AI to count steps.

## Programming guidance: options to test

| Option | Current view | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| QR-linked demonstration video | Likely easiest first candidate; decide after Amanda completes her own programming process | Can a beginner follow it and recover from common mistakes? |
| QR-linked written instructions or reference | Could stand alone or accompany a video | Which steps need searchable detail, screenshots, or a checklist? |
| A prepared prompt for the buyer's own AI | Possible later option | Does it give reliable, accessible help without creating a required subscription? |
| Web app support agent | Possible later option, not MVP | Are recurring problems substantial enough to justify building and maintaining it? |

A video has no live troubleshooting. That may be acceptable for an initial kit; test it with a novice before deciding. A short static FAQ or troubleshooting page is a possible low-effort supplement, not yet an agreed MVP requirement.

## Smallest useful tests

These are proposed experiments, with no agreed pass thresholds yet.

1. **Customer interest:** Show a simple kit concept to prospective beginner makers. Learn whether they want a useful wearable, the making experience, AI-guided learning, or some combination; ask what would stop them buying.
2. **Assembly:** Build the device herself, then have a beginner follow the 3D visual guide with the real parts. Record pauses, errors, questions, and any help needed.
3. **Programming guidance:** Have a beginner program and verify the device using the first QR-linked guide (possibly a video). Record where they get stuck and whether static help is enough before considering an AI prompt or support agent.
4. **Everyday usefulness:** Test whether the wearable stays attached and feels comfortable, compare step counts with manually counted walks, and confirm the buyer can retrieve their data. Amanda's anklet preference is one form factor to explore, not yet the kit design.
5. **Willingness to pay:** Show a mock package or landing page with a proposed price and ask for a meaningful commitment, not just a favourable reaction. Revenue currently has no tested baseline.

The first workshop result can be a demonstrated prototype and evidence from these tests. Selling a finished kit requires later work on repeatable assembly, support, costs, reliability, and product readiness.
