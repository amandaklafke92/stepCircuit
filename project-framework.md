# Working project framework

This is a lightweight, reusable structure for learning through a build. It is not a fixed sequence or a commitment to produce a large set of documents. Revisit a stage when new evidence changes the question.

## The six stages

| Stage | Question | Useful output | When we can move forward |
|---|---|---|---|
| Understand | What do I want to change, and why does it matter? | A short account of motivations, frustrations, and context | We can explain why this project is worth doing without relying on one proposed solution |
| Define success | What would make the result useful, and what do I want to learn? | Outcomes across personal usefulness, learning, and portfolio evidence; primary and stretch goals distinguished | We can recognise a worthwhile first result and identify what still needs a target |
| Explore options | What approaches could work, and what are their trade-offs? | A small set of alternatives and important constraints | We can identify which uncertainties prevent a sensible choice |
| Test uncertainties | What is the smallest experiment that would help us decide? | A question, experiment, observation, and resulting decision | Evidence supports a choice, or tells us what to test next |
| Build and refine | Can the chosen approach work in everyday use? | A working version, real-use feedback, and improvements | The agreed success checks are met, or we explicitly revise the scope |
| Reflect | What did we learn about the product and our way of working? | A brief account of learning and reusable methods | We can explain what to repeat or change on the next project |

Reflection also happens briefly between stages. We do not need to finish the entire product before learning from it.

## Lessons already visible in this project

### Separate motivations from solutions

The need is not that Amanda lacks step counts. It is that her current options involve a recurring subscription or insecure attachment, and that she wants the experience of building. Bracelet, anklet, Bluetooth, and an app are possible ways to address that context.

This distinction prevents us from solving only the most obvious technical problem. Buying another pedometer might solve access and attachment, but it would not fulfil the learning aim.

### Define success across all motivations

Personal usefulness, learning, and portfolio evidence can succeed at different times. An honest, well-documented prototype can be worthwhile before it replaces an everyday tracker. The replacement ambition remains a stretch goal rather than an entry requirement for calling the experiment valuable.

### Turn uncertainty into an experiment

Bracelet versus anklet does not need to be decided through speculation. One possible future test is a removable electronics module in temporary wrist and ankle holders, comparing comfort, security, and counting performance. This is a candidate experiment, not an approved hardware design; the counting software may need adjustment for each placement.

### Capture future ideas without expanding today's work

Automatic updates to a Google Sheet or web app are captured in the success document. Capturing the idea preserves it without committing to an integration before the first wearable and iPhone connection are understood.

## Working partnership

Amanda brings product judgement, systems thinking, personal context, and experience of building web apps. The AI collaborator contributes technical research, explanations, implementation support, and project coordination.

Amanda wants a peer relationship with room to learn. At meaningful decisions, bring options, consequences, and a reasoned recommendation; make clear which statements are constraints, which are hypotheses, and which are preferences. Do not quietly turn a recommendation into a joint decision.

Explain concepts when they help the current decision. At meaningful transitions, briefly name the method just used, why it helped, and another context where it could apply. Keep this proportionate so meta-learning supports the experiment rather than becoming a separate administrative project.

## Lightweight evidence capture

When an experiment or decision occurs, capture only what is useful:

- What question were we trying to answer?
- What did we try or choose, and why?
- What did we observe, including failures or surprises?
- What does that change about our next step?
- What did Amanda contribute, what did AI contribute, and what was independently verified?

Photos, sketches, assembly revisions, and actual test results can support a later portfolio story. Evidence of a result must stay distinct from an intention or an AI-generated demonstration.
