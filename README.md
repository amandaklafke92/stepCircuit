# StepCircuit

A personal wearable step-counter experiment: build something securely worn, access its data without a required subscription, and learn how to take an IoT product from an idea to a working physical object.

Started: 17 September 2026. Project framing began in conversation on 16 September.

## Why this project exists

Amanda's existing trackers already provide step counts. The opportunity is ownership, usability, and learning:

- Her Oura ring tracks steps, but accessing the data involves a subscription (approximately $10/month as reported by Amanda). She questions the recurring cost for this simple function.
- Her clip-on pedometer repeatedly falls off because its clip is unreliable.
- She wants a first IoT build after working mostly on web apps.
- She wants to expand her use of AI and vibe coding, including experimenting with Astra and a Blender assembly walkthrough inspired by a demonstration she saw.
- She wants portfolio evidence showing employers the breadth of her building skills and the judgement behind the result.
- She wants a reusable project process, with timely explanations of frameworks and mental models, so each new project feels less like starting from scratch.

## Project intent

Build a securely worn personal step counter with subscription-free access to its data, using the project to learn IoT development and explore AI-assisted design and assembly instructions.

The working emphasis is a useful learning experiment and credible portfolio project. The stretch goal is to replace Oura for everyday step tracking and automatically update Amanda's Google Sheet and/or web app, removing manual step entry. This does not imply recreating Oura's other functions.

Amanda uses an iPhone. Amanda is leaning towards an anklet; bracelet and integrated centrepiece options remain open pending research and wear testing. No form factor, components, dimensions, budget, app architecture, or implementation schedule has been selected.

## Workshop product concept

**StepCircuit** is the name Amanda chose on 28 September 2026 for this project and its proposed beginner-friendly build-your-own step tracker kit. Her personal wearable experiment informs the kit. The kit concept, MVP support choices, and experiments to test demand and usability are in [StepCircuit product concept](stepcircuit-product-concept.md).
